The living data map is your single "see all my data" surface. Instead of opening ten databases and a hundred spreadsheets, you open one map and understand your whole data landscape at a glance.

---

## What's on the map

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Entities" icon="circle-nodes">
    The real-world things in your data -- customers, orders, products, invoices, contracts, vehicles -- identified automatically from your sources.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Relationships" icon="share-nodes">
    How those things connect: which orders belong to which customer, which documents mention which product, and more.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Sources" icon="plug">
    Where each entity comes from -- which database, file, or document -- so you can always trace a thing back to its origin.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Meaning" icon="book-open">
    A plain-language description of what each entity is and what it's used for, so the map is readable by anyone on your team.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

---

## What you can do with it

- **Understand your data landscape** without being a database expert -- the map is visual and plain-language.
- **Explore by entity type** -- group and filter the map to focus on just customers, just documents, or just one part of your business.
- **Trace anything to its source** -- every entity on the map points back to exactly where it lives.
- **Spot what's connected** -- see at a glance which parts of your business are related, and which are islands.
- **Hand it to your agents** -- the same map your team explores is the understanding your [agents](/agents/overview) use to do their work.

<Info>
  The map is **read-only and safe to explore.** Looking at it never changes your data, and it shows meaning and connections -- not your private business records.
</Info>

---

## It keeps itself current

You don't maintain the map by hand. As your underlying data changes -- new tables, new files, new documents -- İDRAK refreshes the map on a regular schedule, re-checking what exists and how things connect.

When İDRAK re-discovers relationships, it **protects the ones you've confirmed** and only refreshes the rest, so your decisions are never overwritten.

<Tip>
  Just added a big new dataset? Ask DEHA to discover it, and watch it appear on the map -- entities, relationships, and all.
</Tip>
